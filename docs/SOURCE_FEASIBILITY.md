## Study geography

Use the 2025 TIGER/Line county boundary vintage (legal boundaries and names as of **2025-01-01**) for the three owner-selected counties. Census TIGERweb identifies the requested GEOIDs as Boulder, Larimer, and Weld and provides land/water area attributes. Summing the three records gives approximately **7,307 sq mi land + 84 sq mi water = 7,391 sq mi total** (rounded). This is a broad regional footprint that spans Front Range foothills through eastern plains and several flood-map jurisdictions. It is suitable as a contained portfolio-scale geography only with selective acquisition, regional clipping/windowing, and bounded job processing; area alone does not certify operational performance.

The 2025 Census boundary service was not reachable from the workspace and no TIGER geometry file was acquired, unioned, or tested. Thus county IDs, boundary vintage, and area are verified from Census reference metadata, while geometric validity, connectedness, overlap/gaps, and exact polygon-union area remain **unverified**. On first boundary acquisition, validate each polygon and the union, CRS, expected GEOIDs, topological validity, no unintended overlaps/gaps, and stored checksum. This task does not change the approved extent.

Raster load implications (planning estimates, not measured file sizes): at 30 m, the 7,307 land sq mi imply about **21 million land pixels** before tile padding; at nominal 10 m, about **189 million cells**. These are order-of-magnitude counts computed from area and nominal cell size. Do not download full national/time-series products. Acquire only intersecting source tiles/windows; preserve source tiles and metadata as raw objects, and create clipped regional staging products. Choose AOI-level processing windows/masks so every screening does not scan all three-county rasters. Hosted feasibility must be measured from actual intersecting tile sizes, compressed/uncompressed bytes, memory, runtime, and representative AOIs before deployment sizing.

## SSURGO interpretation and suitability

SSURGO is **soil survey information**, not a wetlands inventory. The source’s spatial unit is a soil map-unit polygon; its hydric attributes describe one or more component soils associated with that map unit. NRCS’s schema identifies `component.comppct_r` and `component.hydricrating`, plus `hydricon`; `cohydriccriteria` stores hydric criteria associated with component keys. The checked legacy 2.3.2 schema document establishes the field concepts but the selected current package’s exact schema/version still needs artifact confirmation. Do not refer to `hydricratingpct` as a SSURGO field; use the component proportion field and source-defined rating semantics instead.

For screening, retain ratings and unknown/unranked states and display component-weighted summaries only as an explicitly approximate soil indicator over intersecting map units. Do not paint whole map-unit polygons as hydric solely because one component is rated hydric without exposing that mixed-component simplification. Any derived percentage rule must be transparent and approved as part of the product contract. NRCS notes that map-unit components can include both hydric and non-hydric portions, and a jurisdictional wetland classification additionally requires hydrophytic vegetation and wetland hydrology. SSURGO cannot establish or rule out regulated wetlands; do not state that no wetlands are present when no hydric SSURGO indicator is mapped.

The county-package route is preferred for preserving full-coverage raw data by source survey area. Because Web Soil Survey AOI limits a custom polygon to 100,000 acres, a three-county one-shot custom AOI is not available; use the standard packages for each intersecting SSA or another documented bulk/direct package route, then spatially clip canonical/staging content to the county union. Do not substitute generalized gSSURGO statewide rasters without a later reasoned decision: it changes product granularity and is unnecessary for the component-attribute use case if standard SSURGO packages are manageable.

## FEMA effective/pending and coverage contract

Acquire the effective NFHL snapshot/source data and retain each county/community, FIRM panel, database revision and effective date. Keep FEMA preliminary/pending data as a separately identified non-effective version if ever displayed; do not let it overwrite effective layers before the stated effective date and official confirmation. Larimer County’s announced 2026-11-27 effective date makes this distinction immediately relevant. Census/county boundary coverage is not the same as FEMA study coverage: distinguish mapped, unmapped, not studied, unavailable, and pending states. A missing flood polygon is not evidence of absence of hazard. The screening may report overlap with mapped effective flood zones with the corresponding map date/status, not a regulatory flood determination, insurance decision, or parcel-specific guarantee.
